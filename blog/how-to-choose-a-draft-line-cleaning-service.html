How to Choose a Draft Line Cleaning Service

September 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Draft line cleaning is a service most bar owners never watch. The technician shows up, works for an hour or so, and leaves. Whether your lines are actually clean depends on what happened during that hour. If you're choosing a line cleaning company in Philadelphia, or wondering whether your current one is doing the job, these are the questions that separate a real cleaning from a rinse.

1. How Do You Move the Cleaning Solution Through the Lines?

There are two common approaches. A static clean fills the lines with cleaning solution and lets it sit. A recirculating clean uses a motorized pump to push solution continuously through the lines. The Brewers Association Draught Beer Quality Manual favors recirculation, because moving solution scrubs the inside of the tubing mechanically while the chemistry works. Sitting solution relies on chemistry alone, and buildup that's already established can survive it.

2. What Chemical Do You Use, and at What Strength?

Routine cleaning uses an alkaline (caustic) line cleaner mixed to the manufacturer's recommended concentration and left in contact long enough to work. A company that dilutes its chemical to save money, or rushes contact time, can run through a whole bar quickly and leave the lines barely cleaner than before. Ask what product they use, what concentration they mix it to, and how long it stays in the lines.

3. Do You Take the Faucets and Couplers Apart?

Solution pumped through the lines doesn't reach every surface inside a faucet or coupler. Those parts need to be taken apart, soaked, and scrubbed by hand. Faucets are where yeast and mold show up first, and they're one of the first things an inspector looks at. If the technician never takes a faucet off the tower, part of your system isn't being cleaned.

4. Do You Do Acid Cleaning?

Alkaline cleaners remove organic buildup like biofilm and yeast. They don't remove beer stone, the mineral deposit that forms inside lines over time. That takes an acid cleaning, which the Brewers Association recommends quarterly. A company that only ever runs caustic will leave beer stone behind, and beer stone gives bacteria a place to hide.

5. How Often Will You Come?

Pennsylvania requires dispensing lines, couplers, and related components to be cleaned every 14 days under 40 Pa. Code § 5.51. A schedule that stretches to three or four weeks saves money on paper and puts your license at risk. Ask whether they commit to a fixed 14-day cycle and what happens when a visit gets missed.

6. What Paperwork Do I Get?

Pennsylvania requires you to get a certificate from your cleaner showing the date, the person who cleaned, and the method used, and to keep it on file at your premises. A professional company leaves that record at every visit without being asked. If yours doesn't, your compliance file is empty.

7. Who Is Actually Doing the Work?

Ask about training. Certification programs, such as the Micro Matic Dispense Institute's, cover cleaning method, chemistry, and system troubleshooting. A trained technician also notices problems beyond dirty lines: worn seals, bad temperatures, unbalanced lines, and failing equipment.

Distributor Cleaning vs. an Independent Service

Some distributors offer line cleaning, and some bars rely on it. Under 40 Pa. Code § 5.54, the licensee keeps sole responsibility for the condition of the system no matter who cleans it. Whoever you use, the questions above apply equally.
