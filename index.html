The Method

Why use a recirculating pump to clean beer lines?

There are two methods for cleaning draft beer lines. The pressurized soak fills your lines with chemical and lets it sit for 20 minutes — no agitation, no flow, no mechanical action. It works on short direct-draw systems under 20 feet. For anything longer, it's not enough.

A re-circulating pump is different. The Brewers Association recommends a minimum 15-minute recirculation of cleaning solution — continuously pushed through the line in a closed loop. That constant turbulent flow physically scrubs organic proteins, hop resins, biofilm, yeast, mold, and bacteria from the interior wall of the tubing. Static soaking leaves those deposits behind. Recirculation removes them.

Micro Matic, the industry's leading draft equipment authority, classifies recirculating cleaning as the highly recommended method for all dispensing systems — not just long-draw. The turbulence created by continuous circulation is what makes the difference. A soak has no scrubbing action. A recirculating pump does.

We use the Micro Matic EBC300 — an electric recirculating pump rated for 20 to 300 feet of run. Every cleaning visit delivers full turbulent contact across your entire system. That's what brewery-fresh beer out of your taps requires.
